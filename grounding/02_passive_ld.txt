Category 2: Disputing Passive L&D - Why Traditional Training Fails
Use for: posts on L&D announcements, e-learning webinars, slide-deck trainings, passive AI webinars.
Core argument: Passive lectures yield dismal retention. Active, game-based experiential learning embeds capability.
- Traditional passive training - lectures, slide decks, solitary reading - yields retention between 5% and 10%. You cannot cultivate ethical reasoning or adaptive collaboration by sitting passively.
- Experiential play and game-based learning sit at the base of the Learning Pyramid (practice by doing), driving retention up to 80%-90%.
- Games act as 'failsafe experiments'. They create a low-stakes environment where teams can test bold strategies, experience failures with zero real-world risk, and immediately internalize lessons.
- Complex skills like crisis communication, negotiation, and strategic agility cannot be learned from a slide deck. They need active simulations - learn through doing, failing safely, and reflecting. That is how knowledge becomes embodied capability.
